## 1. Core Principle

The foundational axiom of the **Boundary-Horizon Framework** states that **finite observable structure is an emergent function of the boundary between a system's local constraints and the broader domain that contains it.**

Mathematically, this translates directly to our universal expression:

$$\Omega_{out}(\text{Broader Containing Domain}) - \Omega_{in}(\text{Local System Constraints}) = \mathcal{H}(\text{Horizon Delta}) = N(\text{Finite Observable Structure})$$

Rather than viewing objects, states, or systems as isolated entities, this principle posits that everything observable exists exclusively as a stabilized difference—a finite horizon—forged where containment meets restriction.

## 3. Philosophical & Mathematical Implications

1. **Elimination of Pathological Infinities:** By defining observables as structural deltas ($\Omega_{out} - \Omega_{in}$), infinities and singularities are not physical anomalies to be avoided, but boundary limits where the horizon delta approaches zero ($\mathcal{H} \to 0$).
2. **Universal Morphogenesis:** Whether a system is folding a polypeptide chain into an enzyme or packing 2 meters of DNA into a micron-scale nucleus, form is never arbitrary. It is the precise mechanical solution to the tension between *where it is held* ($\Omega_{out}$) and *what it is made of* ($\Omega_{in}$).
