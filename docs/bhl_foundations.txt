BOUNDARY-HORIZON LOGIC (BHL) FOUNDATIONS

VERSION 0.1

====================================================================

ABSTRACT

Boundary-Horizon Logic (BHL) is a logical framework derived from the
Boundary-Horizon-Finitude (BHF) Framework.

The central premise of BHL is that truth is not the primary object of
logical analysis.

Before a proposition can be evaluated as true or false, it must first
be located relative to an informational boundary.

BHL therefore studies propositions through the concepts of:

- Boundary
- Horizon
- Distinguishability
- Accessibility
- Resolution

The framework is intended as a formal language for reasoning about
incomplete information, self-reference, paradoxes, finite knowledge,
and informational limits.

====================================================================

1. MOTIVATION

Classical logic begins with truth values.

A proposition is assigned one of two values:

TRUE

or

FALSE.

However, many propositions resist immediate classification.

Examples include:

- Russell's Paradox
- The Liar Paradox
- Gödel Sentences
- Unsolved Mathematical Conjectures
- Statements about inaccessible information

Such statements often reveal limitations not of the proposition itself
but of the informational boundary within which evaluation occurs.

BHL therefore asks a prior question:

"Where is the proposition located relative to the evaluating system?"

Only after this question is answered does truth evaluation proceed.

====================================================================

2. THE BOUNDARY PRINCIPLE

A boundary is an informational domain.

Notation:

Ω

Examples of boundaries include:

- A formal logical system
- A mathematical theory
- A physical observer
- A computational model
- A body of scientific knowledge

A boundary determines what can be represented, distinguished, and
resolved within a system.

====================================================================

3. INNER AND OUTER BOUNDARIES

BHL distinguishes between two fundamental domains.

Inner Boundary:

Ωin

The operational domain currently available to a system.

Outer Boundary:

Ωout

The maximal ambient domain containing the system.

The relationship is:

Ωin ⊆ Ωout

A system never operates directly upon all possible information.

It operates only within its current boundary.

====================================================================

4. THE HORIZON PRINCIPLE

The Horizon represents the distinguishable difference between an inner
and outer boundary.

Notation:

H(Ωout, Ωin)

Conceptually:

Horizon = Outer Boundary − Inner Boundary

The Horizon contains information that is:

- Distinguishable
- Accessible in principle
- Not yet fully resolved

The Horizon is therefore the frontier between knowledge and uncertainty.

====================================================================

5. DISTINGUISHABILITY

Distinguishability is the primitive quantity of BHL.

Two objects are distinguishable if a boundary can separate them.

Without distinction:

- No classification exists.
- No identity exists.
- No proposition can be formed.

Thus distinction precedes truth.

Principle:

Distinction -> Representation -> Evaluation

Truth becomes a secondary operation built upon prior distinction.

====================================================================

6. PROPOSITIONS AS BOUNDARY OBJECTS

In BHL, propositions are not initially classified by truth.

Instead they are classified by boundary position.

A proposition p occupies a position relative to a boundary Ω.

Thus:

Position(p, Ω)

is logically prior to

Truth(p).

====================================================================

7. THE SIX INFORMATIONAL STATES

BHL introduces six informational states.

X  = External
U  = Unknown
H  = Horizon
KT = Known True
KF = Known False
UH = Universal Horizon

Descriptions:

X

The proposition lies outside the current domain of discourse.

--------------------------------------------------

U

The proposition exists within the domain but remains inaccessible.

--------------------------------------------------

H

The proposition is distinguishable but unresolved.

--------------------------------------------------

KT

The proposition is resolved as true.

--------------------------------------------------

KF

The proposition is resolved as false.

--------------------------------------------------

UH

The proposition is evaluated relative to the Universal Horizon,
the maximal boundary containing all boundary systems.

====================================================================

8. KNOWLEDGE TRANSITIONS

Knowledge develops through boundary transitions.

Typical progression:

U -> H -> KT

or

U -> H -> KF

A proposition generally becomes:

Unknown

before becoming

Horizon

before becoming

Resolved.

The Horizon therefore functions as an intermediate state between
ignorance and determination.

====================================================================

9. SELF-REFERENCE

Self-reference occurs when evaluation of a proposition requires
evaluation of itself.

Examples include:

"This statement is false."

or

R ∈ R

in Russell's construction.

Classical systems often produce contradiction when unrestricted
self-reference is allowed.

BHL interprets self-reference as evidence that a proposition lies
on a boundary of distinguishability.

====================================================================

10. THE HORIZON CLASSIFICATION PRINCIPLE

BHL introduces the following principle.

If determination of a proposition requires crossing the current
informational boundary, then the proposition occupies a Horizon state.

Symbolically:

Boundary-Crossing(p)

=>

p = H

This allows unresolved propositions to be represented without forcing
immediate contradiction.

====================================================================

11. RUSSELL'S PARADOX

Consider:

R = {x : x not in x}

The question is:

R ∈ R ?

Classical evaluation produces a contradiction.

BHL approaches the statement differently.

The membership question requires recursive self-evaluation.

The proposition therefore exceeds the immediate operational boundary.

Consequently:

R ∈ R = H

The proposition is classified as a Horizon proposition.

The contradiction becomes a boundary phenomenon rather than a truth-value
explosion.

====================================================================

12. THE UNIVERSAL HORIZON

The Universal Horizon is denoted:

UH

UH is not interpreted as a conventional universal set.

Instead UH represents the maximal horizon containing all possible
distinguishable boundary systems.

Every boundary exists within UH.

Formally:

For every Ωi,

Ωi ⊆ UH

UH serves as the ultimate context within which all local boundaries
can be compared.

====================================================================

13. THE BOUNDARY-HORIZON VIEW OF LOGIC

Classical Logic asks:

"Is the proposition true or false?"

Boundary-Horizon Logic asks:

"Where is the proposition located relative to a boundary?"

The evaluation process therefore becomes:

1. Locate the proposition.
2. Determine accessibility.
3. Determine distinguishability.
4. Determine resolution.
5. Assign truth status if possible.

Truth is thus derived from boundary position rather than assumed
as a primitive concept.

====================================================================

14. CORE FOUNDATIONAL AXIOMS

Axiom F1: Boundary Existence

Every logical system possesses at least one informational boundary.

--------------------------------------------------

Axiom F2: Boundary Inclusion

Every operational boundary exists within a larger ambient boundary.

Ωin ⊆ Ωout
